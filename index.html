What we do

Check the bill

We recompute an Eskom electricity bill line by line from the published tariff rules, and show exactly where the bill and the rules disagree.

Check the deal

Renewable power delivered over the grid, known as wheeling, is sold on claimed savings of 20 to 30%. We provide the independent benchmark that tests those claims, including the network charges the sales spreadsheets tend to leave out.

Make the market measurable

Southern Africa's traded electricity prices exist only inside PDF reports. We turn them into usable data, and we will be measuring South Africa's new wholesale market when it opens.

Every figure is traced back to the document it came from, so buyers, lenders and traders know what power actually costs, and whether a promised saving is real.

The bill review

We recompute one Eskom invoice line by line from the published tariff rules and NERSA's decisions, and report every line where the bill and the rules disagree.

What we need
The figures only, not the invoice document:
  • Kilowatt-hours by time-of-use period and season
  • Chargeable demand and utilised capacity, in kilovolt-amperes
  • Days in the billing period and number of points of delivery
  • Voltage band and transmission distance zone
  • The charge lines, excluding Value-Added Tax
What comes back
The reconciliation, line by line: what the published rules produce for each charge, what the invoice charged, and the variance between them.
Turnaround
Five working days from receiving the figures.
Cost
Free, with no obligation. The result is yours to keep.

Two lines disagree here. The network demand charge has been applied to utilised capacity rather than to chargeable demand, and the ancillary service charge to a kilowatt-hour total that is not the one metered. Both are basis errors rather than wrong rates, which is the kind that survives a look down the invoice.

A finding we have published

Eskom's 2026/27 generation capacity charge is about 1.5 times the 2025/26 rate at every voltage band. That is not the 8.76% annual increase. It is a reallocation between fixed and variable recovery, set out in NERSA's Reasons for Decision.

The part that matters to a wheeling model is separate. NERSA excludes the charge from the energy credit under wheeling and net-billing, so a model that credits any part of it back to the customer overstates the saving.

How much that moves any particular bill depends on that customer's load factor and consumption. It is arithmetic, and it has to be done per customer, which is what the bill review does.

The 20% that isn't a discount

Eskom's generation capacity charge is commonly summarised as phased in at 20% then 30%. Customers pay the full charge in every year, and no part of it can be reduced by wheeling.

About

Datum Markets is one person. David Jinadu is an ACCA accountant who rebuilds power market numbers from published primary sources, working from the United Kingdom on southern African markets.

There is no data feed behind this and no panel of contributors. Every figure comes from a document anyone can download: a regulator's decision, a published tariff schedule, a market operator's report. Each one carries the reference it came from, so a reader can check it rather than take it on trust. Where a number cannot be traced to a published document, it is not used.
