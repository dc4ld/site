We check what electricity really costs.

Businesses in southern Africa spend millions on power through bills and contracts that almost nobody independently verifies. Datum Markets rebuilds the numbers from the regulator's and the utility's own published documents.

What we do

Check the bill

We recompute an Eskom electricity bill line by line from the published tariff rules, and show exactly where the bill and the rules disagree.

Check the deal

Renewable power delivered over the grid, known as wheeling, is sold on claimed savings of 20 to 30%. We provide the independent benchmark that tests those claims, including the network charges the sales spreadsheets tend to leave out.

Make the market measurable

Southern Africa's traded electricity prices exist only inside PDF reports. We turn them into usable data, and we will be measuring South Africa's new wholesale market when it opens.

Every figure is traced back to the document it came from, so buyers, lenders and traders know what power actually costs, and whether a promised saving is real.

Analysis

The 20% that isn't a discount

Eskom's generation capacity charge is commonly summarised as phased in at 20% then 30%. Customers pay the full charge in every year, and no part of it can be reduced by wheeling.

About

Datum Markets is one person. David Jinadu is a chartered accountant who rebuilds power market numbers from published primary sources, working from the United Kingdom on southern African markets.

There is no data feed behind this and no panel of contributors. Every figure comes from a document anyone can download: a regulator's decision, a published tariff schedule, a market operator's report. Each one carries the reference it came from, so a reader can check it rather than take it on trust. Where a number cannot be traced to a published document, it is not used.
